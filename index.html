About Me

Who I Am

I'm a mechanical engineer who has spent my career at the intersection of design, data, and hands-on problem solving. I started at OMG Roofing Products as an R&D intern back in 2021, came back full-time as an R&D Engineer after finishing my Master's in Mechatronics, and now work as a New Product Development Engineer, building the fasteners and systems that hold roofs together. Along the way I picked up a love of teaching too — in 2025 I spent a semester as adjunct faculty at Western New England University, running the Machine Vision and SPC lab for junior engineering students, and in 2022 I presented a paper on low-cost autonomous mobile robots at the ASME IMECE conference.

Outside of work, I'm usually behind a camera, on a pair of skis, or designing something small enough to 3D print on my desk. I held a private pilot license from 2021 to 2025, which fed the same curiosity that got me into engineering in the first place.

Featured Work

Product Development

RhinoMark Plate Marking System

OMG Roofing Products

Developed and launched a plate-marking tool that helps roofers quickly locate and mark RhinoBond plates beneath the membrane. Magnetic guides center the tool over each plate and prevent marking unless it's properly aligned, cutting down on rework, protecting the roof, and keeping the finished surface clean.

Robotics & Construction Automation

OMG Roofing Products

Helped adapt an existing commercial roofing installation tool into an autonomous mobile robot (AMR). Tested GPS position accuracy and its limitations on large AMRs, along with basic obstacle detection.

Machine Learning

AI-Based Machine Condition Monitoring System

ASME IMECE 2024 Conference Paper · with Dr. Vedang Chauhan & Sunny Panchal

Built a condition-monitoring system for vibratory bowl feeders, combining Cognex machine vision (edge learning for jam and clog detection) with machine learning models — Random Forest, Gradient Boosting, SVM, and MLP — trained on sensor data from a custom test rig. Used Design of Experiments to capture data across a wide range of operating conditions, and found frequency-domain vibration data pushed SVM and GBDT models past 95% F1-score, far outperforming time-domain data.

Low-Cost Autonomous Mobile Robot for Outdoor Applications
